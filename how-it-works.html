how it works

from “how will I pay rent?”
to funded, in about 48 hours.

No credit check. No employment requirement. No repayment unless your case wins. Here’s exactly what happens after you apply — every step, in plain English.

Tell us about your case

Fill out our five-minute application online, or call us and we’ll take it over the phone. We’ll ask about your injury, your attorney, and roughly where the case stands. That’s it — no documents required from you, no fee to apply, and applying never obligates you to accept anything.

~5 minutes

We call your attorney

With your permission, our underwriters — several of whom practiced as personal injury attorneys — review the case directly with your lawyer: liability, insurance coverage, damages, and realistic timeline. You don’t gather paperwork or chase anyone. We handle it.

Same day, in most cases

Get a one-page offer

If we can fund your case, you’ll get a single clear page: the advance amount, the fixed repayment schedule, and the 2.0× cap — the absolute maximum you could ever repay, no matter how long your case takes. Take it to your attorney, ask us anything, and decide on your own timeline.

Within 24 hours

Money in your account

Sign electronically and choose wire or overnight check. Then go back to what matters: your recovery. You’ll make no payments while the case is pending — repayment comes out of settlement proceeds, through your attorney, at the very end.

24–48 hours after signing

what you’ll repay

one number, capped forever

Most funders quote a rate and let compounding do the rest. We don’t. Your agreement shows a fixed repayment schedule with a hard ceiling of 2.0× the amount advanced — even if your case takes five years.

  • No compounding — the schedule in your offer is the whole story.
  • No application, processing, or “administrative” fees.
  • Case resolves early? You repay less, per the same schedule.
  • Case doesn’t win? You owe nothing. Full stop.

“A $10,000 advance can never cost you more than $20,000 — not in year three, not in year five, not ever.”
