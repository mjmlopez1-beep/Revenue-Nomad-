The Digital Front Door Index

For most patients, care starts with a phone call or a website. Every year we call U.S. health systems the way a patient would and inspect their websites the way a new patient would use them. Then we score how easy it is to get through. This is the national edition, covering all 50 states.

    Chapter 02

    Phone access

    Field test · measured

    What one day of calls to Oregon clinics actually showed

    On September 21, 2026, we called the published numbers of 575 clinic lines at 25 Oregon health systems, hospitals and community health centers, once mid-morning and again in the early afternoon. We timed each call from the first ring to the first word from a live person. This is the kind of measurement every state in the index will be built on.

    Fast pickup and endless hold in the same organization.One large system had a median wait under 7 seconds. It also accounted for 30 of the 38 lines that held us for the full 20 minutes. Averages hide this, and line-by-line data shows it.
    Time of day changes the answer.The same line answered in 10 seconds mid-morning and 77 seconds in the early afternoon. Another answered in 1 second in the morning and dropped the call after lunch. Of the 64 lines we called in both windows, several behaved this way.

    Chapter 07

    About this index

    The Digital Front Door score runs from 0 to 100. It combines four measures of access, each weighted by how often patients depend on it. Every rule below is applied the same way to every system.

    How we measured

    • Calls: an automated voice agent called each system's published clinic numbers, the numbers a patient would find. Each line got one mid-morning and one early-afternoon call on a weekday, plus one call to the main line after 6 p.m. local time.
    • Wait time: the time from the start of the call to the first word spoken by a live person, read from the call transcript. Calls stop at 20 minutes.
    • Websites: the home page and location finder, checked as a new patient on a phone and a laptop, plus an automated accessibility scan.
    • No staff were interviewed and no call recordings are published.

    Grades

    Independence and disclosure

    The index is published by Clarus Care, which sells call-handling software to health systems. To keep the results neutral, the scoring rules are published in full, every system is measured with the same scripts on the same schedule, and customers are scored the same way as everyone else. No system can pay to be included, excluded or re-scored.

    Limitations

    • Each line is called on one day, so a single score is a snapshot. Treat differences of a few points as noise.
    • Published numbers are not always the lines a system wants patients to use. We score the experience a patient actually gets.
    • The accessibility scan is a signal, not a full conformance audit.
    • Small states and small peer groups have wide ranges. Pages flag any comparison based on fewer than 20 systems.

    Corrections

    Any system can request its line-level call data and ask for a re-measurement if a published number or listing is wrong.
