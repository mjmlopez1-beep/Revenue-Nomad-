The Digital Front Door Index 2026

For most patients, care starts with a phone call or a website. We measured how easy that first step is at US health systems and FQHCs, and the gap between the best and the rest is wider than most leaders assume.

Published October 2026 · systems across 50 states · Methodology

Field test · measured

What one day of calls to Oregon clinics actually showed

On September 21, 2026, we called the published numbers of 575 clinic lines across 25 Oregon health systems, hospitals and FQHCs, in a mid-morning and an early-afternoon window. No staff were spoken to. We timed each call from the first ring to the first word from a live person. This is the kind of evidence behind every state in the Index.

What this tells a COO: the same line can be fine at 10 a.m. and dead at 1 p.m. Of the 64 lines we dialed in both windows, several answered in seconds in the morning and then went unanswered or dropped the call in the afternoon. One large system had a median wait under 7 seconds, yet it accounted for 30 of the 38 lines that left us in a queue for the full 20 minutes. A system-wide average hides individual lines that are failing patients.

Methodology

How the Index works

Each system gets four pillar scores from 0 to 100, rolled into a single DFD Score. Each pillar also gets a letter grade from A to F. Phone metrics come from calls placed by an AI voice agent to published clinic numbers. Digital and accessibility metrics come from inspecting each system's public website. The full definitions are in the methodology notes.
